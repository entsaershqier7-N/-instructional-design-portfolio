# Phishing Defense — Design Document

## 1. Performance Problem
Employees may know basic security rules but still click or respond when a message creates urgency, authority, or fear.

## 2. Target Audience
General employees in hybrid or remote work environments.

## 3. Instructional Goal
Enable learners to perform the target behaviors confidently in realistic workplace situations.

## 4. Learning Objectives
- Identify at least four warning signs in a suspicious email or message.
- Choose the safest response to a suspected phishing attempt.
- Apply a simple verification process before acting on urgent requests.

## 5. Instructional Strategy
- Use short content segments followed by application.
- Favor realistic decisions over recall-only questions.
- Give immediate, explanatory feedback.
- Keep navigation simple and accessible.
- Provide a job aid for use after training.

## 6. Course Outline
1. Why phishing works
2. Four red flags
3. STOP–CHECK–VERIFY–REPORT
4. Guided practice
5. Final challenge

## 7. Assessment Strategy
Three scenario decisions plus a five-question final challenge. Passing score: 80%.

## 8. Evaluation Plan
Kirkpatrick L1: confidence and relevance; L2: assessment accuracy; L3: simulated phishing behavior 30–60 days later.

## 9. Accessibility Considerations
- Keyboard-accessible buttons and links.
- High contrast text and simple layouts.
- Meaning is never communicated by color alone.
- Plain-language instructions.
- Responsive layout for smaller screens.

## 10. Prototype Notes
This portfolio version is a functional HTML/CSS/JavaScript prototype intended to demonstrate the instructional strategy and interaction design.
